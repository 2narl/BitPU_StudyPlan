This study plan is for **Year I, Semester I** of the Bachelor of Information Technology (BIT) program under Purbanchal University (PU). It follows the organization and study approach used in the **Second Semester** folder of this repository.

> **Syllabus note:** The course codes, titles, credits and weekly teaching-hour allocations below are based on the PU first-year course structure. The detailed outlines in the subject files combine the syllabus topics available to us with expanded learning checklists for study. Where the complete current PU course specification was not available, the outline is explicitly marked as a study guide and should be checked against the latest handout issued by your college.

# Overall Semester Strategy

## Daily Study Time (4–5 Hours)

| Subject | Suggested Time |
|---|---:|
| C Programming (theory + coding) | 1 hour 15 minutes |
| Mathematics-I | 1 hour |
| Fundamentals of IT | 45 minutes |
| Technical Communication | 30 minutes |
| Society and Ethics in IT | 30 minutes |
| Project-I / practical work | 30 minutes |
| Revision | 15 minutes |

Adjust the plan according to college classes, assignment deadlines and your own weak areas. Give extra time to C programming and Mathematics because they improve through regular practice.

## How to Study Each Subject

- **Fundamentals of IT:** Learn definitions, compare related concepts, draw diagrams and connect each topic with real-world examples.
- **Mathematics-I:** Understand formulas and conditions, solve examples step by step, and practise numerical problems every day.
- **Technical Communication:** Practise speaking, reading comprehension, note-making, formal letters, proposals and reports.
- **Society and Ethics in IT:** Use examples and case studies to understand the social, legal and ethical effects of technology.
- **C Programming:** Write, compile, test and debug programs yourself. Do not only read or copy code.
- **Project-I:** Maintain a project notebook, define a small problem, document each development stage and prepare a demonstration.

# Monthly Revision Plan

### Week 1
- Study the introductory units of all subjects.
- Set up a C compiler and practise basic programs.
- Create a formula notebook for Mathematics-I.

### Week 2
- Continue the next units.
- Solve mathematics exercises and write C programs without copying.
- Practise reading comprehension and formal writing.

### Week 3
- Revise completed topics.
- Complete practical exercises and project milestones.
- Attempt questions from available past papers.

### Week 4
- Take a timed mock test for each theory subject.
- Review mistakes and revise weak topics.
- Demonstrate project progress and update documentation.

---

# Exam Preparation (Last Month)

- Complete the syllabus before the final revision period.
- Prepare short notes for definitions, comparisons, formulas and diagrams.
- Solve at least five available previous-year papers for each theory subject.
- Practise C programs by writing them from memory and testing them on a compiler.
- Practise mathematics problems under time limits.
- Prepare examples and case studies for Society and Ethics in IT.
- Revise technical writing formats and practise timed writing.
- Keep actual past questions separate from predicted questions.

## Final Advice

Build strong foundations in Mathematics-I, Fundamentals of IT and C Programming. At the same time, do not ignore communication, digital citizenship or project work. A steady routine—reading, solving, coding, writing and revising every week—is more effective than preparing all subjects at the last minute.
